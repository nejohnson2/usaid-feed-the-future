# Household-Level Data Sources for Poverty, Hunger, and Malnutrition: A Literature Review of 20 Former Feed the Future Countries

**Prepared: March 2026**

---

## 1. Introduction

The Feed the Future (FTF) initiative, launched by USAID in 2010, established a data-driven approach to measuring poverty, hunger, and malnutrition across 20 target countries. Although the initiative has evolved, the need for comprehensive, nationally representative household data to inform analytics on food security and nutrition outcomes remains critical. This review identifies and catalogs the major household-level data sources available for each of the 20 former FTF countries over the past 20 years (2005-2026), covering demographic and health surveys, living standards measurements, nutrition assessments, food security analyses, agricultural surveys, and the FTF Zone of Influence (ZOI) population-based surveys themselves.

The 20 countries span four regions:
- **East Africa:** Kenya, Ethiopia, Uganda, Rwanda, Tanzania
- **Southern/Southeast Africa & Liberia:** Zambia, Mozambique, Malawi, Madagascar, Liberia
- **West Africa:** Ghana, Nigeria, Mali, Senegal, Niger
- **South Asia & Latin America:** Nepal, Bangladesh, Guatemala, Honduras
- **Central Africa:** Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC)

## 2. Major Cross-Country Survey Programs

### 2.1 Demographic and Health Surveys (DHS)

The DHS Program (dhsprogram.com) remains the single most important source of nationally representative data on health, nutrition, and population across all 20 countries. Every country in this review has conducted at least 2 DHS rounds since 2005, with most completing 3-5 rounds. DHS surveys collect anthropometric measurements (stunting, wasting, underweight), dietary diversity, food security indicators, and wealth index data at the household level. Data require free registration but are otherwise openly accessible.

**Notable patterns:**
- **Senegal** adopted a unique "Continuous DHS" model after 2010, producing annual or biannual datasets from 2012 through 2019 and again in 2023 -- the most frequent DHS data collection of any country in this review.
- **Ethiopia** and **Rwanda** have 5 DHS rounds each (including interim/mini surveys).
- **Niger's** 2017 DHS was suppressed due to data quality concerns, creating a significant gap between the 2012 and any future DHS round.
- Several countries have very recent or ongoing DHS rounds: **Zambia** (2024), **Malawi** (2024), **Ethiopia** (2024-25), **DRC** (2023-24), **Nigeria** (2023-24), and **Mali** (2023-24).

### 2.2 Multiple Indicator Cluster Surveys (MICS)

MICS (mics.unicef.org), implemented by UNICEF, provides comparable indicators on child health, nutrition, education, and protection. Coverage varies considerably across the 20 countries:

- **Nigeria** has the most extensive MICS program with 5 rounds (2007, 2011, 2016-17, 2021, 2023-24 MICS Plus).
- **Ghana** has 3 MICS rounds (2006, 2011, 2017-18).
- **Malawi**, **Mali**, and **Bangladesh** each have 2 rounds.
- **Kenya** conducted only subnational MICS surveys (Eastern Province, Nyanza, specific counties).
- **Niger** integrated MICS modules within its DHS surveys (EDSN-MICS format) rather than conducting standalone MICS.
- **Ethiopia**, **Rwanda**, and **Tanzania** have not conducted MICS in this period, relying on DHS for comparable indicators.
- **Nepal** has the most frequent MICS schedule among this group with rounds in 2010, 2014, 2019, and 2024-25.

### 2.3 Living Standards Measurement Study - Integrated Surveys on Agriculture (LSMS-ISA)

The World Bank's LSMS-ISA program (microdata.worldbank.org) provides multi-topic panel household surveys with strong agricultural components. This is one of the richest sources for poverty and food security analysis, and all data are publicly downloadable.

**LSMS-ISA partner countries in this review:**
- **Uganda** -- National Panel Survey (UNPS), 7+ waves from 2009-2020
- **Ethiopia** -- Socioeconomic Survey (ESS), 5 waves from 2011-2022
- **Tanzania** -- National Panel Survey (NPS), 5 waves from 2008-2021
- **Nigeria** -- General Household Survey Panel (GHS-Panel), 5 waves from 2010-2024 (the most recent)
- **Malawi** -- Integrated Household Surveys (IHS3-IHS5) plus panel follow-ups, 2004-2020
- **Mali** -- Enquete Agricole de Conjoncture Integree (EAC-I), 2 waves (2014-15, 2017-18)
- **Niger** -- ECVM/A, 2 waves (2011, 2014)

Countries without LSMS-ISA coverage include Kenya, Rwanda, Ghana, Senegal, Liberia, Madagascar, Zambia, Mozambique, Nepal, Bangladesh, Guatemala, Honduras, and DRC.

### 2.4 Harmonized Household Living Conditions Surveys (EHCVM)

Three West African WAEMU member countries -- **Mali**, **Niger**, and **Senegal** -- participate in the EHCVM harmonized survey program, which enables cross-country poverty comparisons. Two rounds have been completed (2018-19 and 2021-22), all publicly available via the World Bank Microdata Catalog.

### 2.5 IPC/Cadre Harmonise Food Security Classifications

All 20 countries participate in either the IPC (Integrated Food Security Phase Classification) or the Cadre Harmonise (CH) system for West Africa. These provide area-level food security classifications rather than household-level microdata, but they are important contextual resources. The five West African countries (Ghana, Nigeria, Mali, Senegal, Niger) use the Cadre Harmonise system with biannual analyses. All reports are publicly available at ipcinfo.org.

### 2.6 Feed the Future Zone of Influence Surveys

All 20 countries had FTF population-based surveys conducted in their designated Zones of Influence. Phase 1 surveys typically included a baseline (~2012-13), interim assessment (~2015), and endline (~2018-19). Several countries also had Phase 2 baselines (~2019-20). These datasets are publicly downloadable via the USAID Development Data Library (data.usaid.gov).

**Notable FTF data:**
- **Bangladesh** used the IFPRI Bangladesh Integrated Household Survey (BIHS) as its FTF vehicle, with 3 waves (2011-12, 2015, 2018-19) publicly available on Harvard Dataverse.
- **Madagascar** and **DRC** were added as FTF target countries more recently (2022), with newer baseline surveys.
- **Tanzania** leveraged its LSMS NPS Wave 4 data alongside supplemental FTF surveys.

## 3. National Household Income and Expenditure Surveys

Every country maintains its own national household income/expenditure survey program, though frequency and data accessibility vary considerably:

**Most frequent coverage (every 2-4 years):**
- **Rwanda** -- EICV series, 6 rounds from 2005-2024 (every ~3 years)
- **Uganda** -- UNHS, 6 rounds from 2005-2024
- **Bangladesh** -- HIES, 4 rounds from 2005-2022
- **Honduras** -- EPHPM, conducted annually since 2005 (the most frequent)

**Moderate coverage (every 4-6 years):**
- **Nepal** -- NLSS, 3 rounds (2003-04, 2010-11, 2022-23)
- **Ghana** -- GLSS, 3 rounds (2005-06, 2012-13, 2016-17)
- **Guatemala** -- ENCOVI, 4 rounds (2006, 2011, 2014, 2023)
- **Zambia** -- LCMS, 6 rounds from 2004-2022
- **Ethiopia** -- HICES/HCE, 3 rounds (2004-05, 2010-11, 2015-16)
- **Tanzania** -- HBS, 4 rounds (2007, 2011-12, 2017-18, 2024-25)
- **Kenya** -- KIHBS, 2 completed rounds (2005-06, 2015-16) with a third underway (2025-26)

**Limited coverage or access challenges:**
- **DRC** -- Enquete 1-2-3 with only 2 rounds (2004-05, 2012) and a new ECVM planned for 2024
- **Liberia** -- HIES with 2 rounds (2014-15, 2016-17) and a new round underway in 2025
- **Madagascar** -- EPM with 3 rounds (2005, 2010, 2021-22)
- **Mozambique** -- IOF with 4 rounds (2008-09, 2014-15, 2019-20, 2022-23)

## 4. National Nutrition Surveys

Nutrition data quality and frequency vary substantially:

**Countries with annual SMART nutrition surveys:**
- **Mali** -- annual since 2011 (one of the longest-running programs in West Africa)
- **Niger** -- annual since 2006 (the most extensive in West Africa)
- **Malawi** -- 8 SMART rounds between 2015-2024

**Countries with periodic national nutrition surveys:**
- **Nepal** -- National Micronutrient Status Survey (2016), new survey planned for 2026
- **Bangladesh** -- National Micronutrient Surveys (2011-12, 2013)
- **Ethiopia** -- National Food Consumption Survey (2011), Micronutrient Survey (2015), Food & Nutrition Strategy Baseline (2021-23)
- **Ghana** -- Micronutrient Survey (2017)
- **Nigeria** -- NNHS-SMART surveys (2014, 2015, 2018)
- **Tanzania** -- TNNS (2014, 2018)
- **Zambia** -- Food Consumption & Micronutrient Status Surveys (2014, 2020, 2023)
- **Guatemala** -- ENMICRON (2009-10); SIVESNU surveillance system (2013-2019)

**Countries relying primarily on DHS/MICS for nutrition data:**
- **Uganda**, **Kenya** (with county-level SMART surveys in ASAL areas), **Honduras**, **Liberia** (integrated in CFSNS)

**Countries with subnational SMART surveys only:**
- **DRC** -- extensive provincial SMART surveys through PRONANUT but no comprehensive national nutrition survey
- **Madagascar** -- subnational SMART surveys, particularly in the south
- **Mozambique** -- subnational SMART surveys, particularly in conflict-affected northeast

**Rwanda** takes a unique approach by integrating nutrition data into its regular CFSVA program (8 rounds since 2006, every 2-3 years), providing one of the most consistent food security and nutrition monitoring datasets of any country in this review.

## 5. Food Security Assessments

Beyond the IPC/CH classifications, several countries have conducted WFP Comprehensive Food Security and Vulnerability Analyses (CFSVAs):

- **Rwanda** stands out with 8 CFSVA rounds (2006, 2009, 2012, 2015, 2018, 2021, 2022, 2024)
- **Mozambique** was the first country to implement all three IPC scales (Acute, Chronic, and Acute Malnutrition)
- **DRC** is one of the most heavily IPC-analyzed countries globally
- **Ghana** has 2 CFSVA rounds (2012, 2020)
- **Liberia** has the most integrated approach with its CFSNS series (2006, 2008, 2010, 2018, 2025)

## 6. Agricultural Surveys

Agricultural data availability ranges from annual surveys to multi-decade gaps:

**Annual agricultural survey programs:**
- **Ethiopia** -- Annual Agricultural Sample Survey (AgSS), continuously since the 1980s
- **Rwanda** -- Seasonal Agricultural Survey (SAS), annually since 2012
- **Senegal** -- Enquete Agricole Annuelle (EAA), expanded with AGRISurvey methodology since 2017
- **Mali** -- Enquete Agricole de Conjoncture (EAC), annual

**Periodic agricultural censuses or surveys:**
- **Tanzania** -- Censuses in 2007-08 and 2019-20, plus AASS 2022-23
- **Malawi** -- Agricultural censuses 2006-07 and 2020 (with LSMS-ISA ag modules in between)
- **Uganda** -- Census 2008-09, then Annual Agricultural Survey since 2017
- **Mozambique** -- Extensive TIA/IAI series with 11+ rounds from 2002-2020 (harmonized dataset available through UNU-WIDER)
- **Nigeria** -- NASC 2022 and NASS 2023 (plus GHS-Panel ag modules)
- **Nepal** -- Agricultural censuses in 2011-12 and 2021-22
- **Ghana** -- Census of Agriculture 2017-18
- **Guatemala** -- Encuesta Nacional Agropecuaria (annual ~2005-2014)

**Countries with significant gaps:**
- **Kenya** -- No comprehensive agricultural census since the 1960s until the 2024-25 pilot
- **DRC** -- No national agricultural census conducted in the 2005-2025 period
- **Liberia** -- Only the second agricultural census since 1971 was conducted in 2024 (digitally)
- **Honduras** -- First census in 32 years conducted in 2024-25

## 7. Cross-Cutting Observations and Data Gaps

### Data-Rich Countries
**Malawi** has arguably the richest household data landscape, driven by its LSMS-ISA panel surveys (IHS3-IHS5 with integrated agricultural modules), combined with regular DHS, MICS, SMART, and IPC assessments. **Nigeria** benefits from the longest GHS-Panel series (5 waves through 2024) and frequent MICS rounds. **Rwanda** and **Uganda** stand out for the regularity of their national household surveys and food security monitoring.

### Notable Data Gaps
- **Niger** has a critical DHS gap due to the suppression of the 2017 survey, leaving a 10+ year gap between the 2012 and any future DHS round.
- **DRC** faces significant challenges with limited national-level household surveys (only 2 Enquete 1-2-3 rounds) and no national nutrition survey, though subnational data is extensive.
- **Kenya** had a major gap in agricultural data collection before the 2024-25 census pilot.
- **Honduras** has limited standalone nutrition survey data.

### Emerging Trends
- The **FAO 50x2030 Initiative** is expanding agricultural data collection in several countries (Liberia, Niger, Senegal) through the AGRISurvey methodology.
- **COVID-19 phone surveys** conducted during 2020-2022 by the World Bank in several countries (Ethiopia, Kenya, Uganda, Tanzania, Nigeria) include food security modules and provide high-frequency data during the pandemic period.
- **MICS Plus** phone-based surveys (Nigeria 2023-24) represent a new approach to more frequent data collection between traditional survey rounds.
- Several countries are now in the process of collecting their most recent rounds of major surveys (Zambia DHS 2024, Malawi DHS 2024, Ethiopia DHS 2024-25, Kenya KIHBS 2025-26, Tanzania IHBS 2024-25).

## 9. Conclusion

The data landscape across these 20 countries has expanded significantly over the past two decades. DHS remains the most consistent and comparable cross-country data source, with all 20 countries maintaining regular survey cycles. The LSMS-ISA program provides the deepest household panel data, but covers only 7 of the 20 countries. National household survey programs vary widely in frequency -- from Honduras's annual EPHPM to DRC's sporadic Enquete 1-2-3 series.

For poverty analytics, the combination of DHS (health/nutrition), LSMS-ISA or national HIES (consumption/expenditure), and IPC/CH (food security classification) provides the most comprehensive picture. The Feed the Future ZOI surveys, while geographically limited to zones of influence rather than nationally representative, offer uniquely targeted food security and resilience indicators. Agricultural survey availability has improved markedly in recent years, with several countries conducting their first comprehensive agricultural censuses in decades.

Researchers and analysts seeking to build a comprehensive poverty, hunger, and malnutrition analytical framework should prioritize the DHS and LSMS-ISA/national HIES datasets as foundational sources, supplemented by MICS, SMART/nutrition surveys, and IPC/CH analyses for additional dimensions and temporal coverage.
